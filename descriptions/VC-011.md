> 🏠 **The core question:** given this specific property at this price, which
> investment strategy generates the best returns — and how sensitive are those
> returns to changes in your assumptions?

Every property buyer asks that. Almost nobody answers it with numbers.
DealScope takes one property, runs it through four exit strategies side by
side, and produces a written report rather than a bare percentage.

## 🏘️ Four Strategies, Modeled Side by Side

| Strategy | Driven by |
|---|---|
| 🛏️ **Short-term rental** | Nightly rate, occupancy curve, platform fees, cleaning, utilities, furnishing, licensing |
| 🔑 **Long-term rental** | Monthly rent, vacancy, tenant default, property tax, condo fees, insurance, maintenance |
| 🔨 **Buy-renovate-resell** | Purchase → works per m² → holding costs → selling costs → capital-gains tax |
| 📝 **Off-plan assignment** | Deposits actually paid in, developer consent fee, transfer-tax saving, completion date |

The shared engine is what makes the comparison honest: strategies differ
because the strategies differ, not because each was given its own quietly
flattering assumptions.

---

## 📊 Ranges, Not Point Estimates

- **Three cases in parallel** — pessimistic, base, optimistic
- **Tornado charts** — which assumptions actually drive the outcome, ranked
- **Monte Carlo (optional)** — the *probability* of clearing your target return

> ⚠️ This is the part that changes decisions. A deal commonly looks best as a
> short-term rental in the base case and worst in the downside, purely because
> the model is levered to occupancy.

---

## 📐 Reported for Every Strategy

Net yield · cash-on-cash · IRR · NPV · payback period · equity multiple

...plus the strategy-specific break-even: the occupancy rate, monthly rent or
resale price at which the deal stops making money.

## 📄 Output: A Real Report

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| 📋 Assumptions | Every input, with its source |
| ⚖️ Comparison | All four strategies on identical metrics |
| 💧 Cash flow | Per-scenario waterfalls |
| 📉 Sensitivity | Tornado + break-even charts |
| ✅ Verdict | Plain language, **with conditions** |

> **Example verdict:** works as a long-term rental at the asking price — works
> as a short-term rental only above 65% occupancy.

---

## 👥 Two Audiences, Opposite Sides of the Table

- 🏢 **Estate agents** — advisory: educate the client with data rather than photos
- 🧾 **Buyers** — negotiating armour: comparables ranked, asking-price percentile

Serving both only works if the tool refuses to take a side. The report says the
same thing regardless of who ran it — the instant a conclusion depends on who
paid for it, it is worth nothing to either party.

## 🆓 Truly Free

- ❌ No paywalls, no blurred sections, no account required to run an analysis
- 💛 An optional donation button, appearing **only after** the report is generated
- 👤 An optional account, solely to store past analyses — never a requirement

---

## 🔒 Data Governance

- 🗄️ Every analysis stored with full privacy controls
- ⏳ Retention windows rather than indefinite storage
- 🔀 Identifying data separated from deal metrics, so aggregate market insight publishes without exposing anyone
- 🗑️ Deletion honoured as a working feature, not a policy paragraph

> ⚠️ Letting an anonymous visitor email a branded PDF anywhere is, viewed
> unkindly, a well-designed spam cannon. Verified senders, per-day recipient
> caps, scanned cover notes and a dedicated sending subdomain are day-one
> requirements, not hardening.

---

## 🌍 Jurisdiction-Specific Rules

Transfer tax, capital gains, rental income treatment and short-let licensing
live in per-country config files, never hardcoded — they differ everywhere and
change with every budget.

---

## 🧭 Design Philosophy

> The tool shows all its work. Every assumption is visible, sourced and
> editable.

Explicitly a modeling aid and **not financial advice**, designed so a
professional reviewing the report can see exactly which number to challenge —
rather than being asked to trust a black box.
