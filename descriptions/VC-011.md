> **The core question:** given this specific property at this price, which
> investment strategy generates the best returns — and how sensitive are
> those returns to changes in your assumptions?

Every property buyer asks that. Almost nobody answers it with numbers.
DealScope takes one property, runs it through four different exit strategies
side by side, and produces a written report on the deal rather than a bare
percentage.

---

## 🏘️ Four Strategies, Modeled Side by Side

- 🛏️ **Short-term rental (Airbnb)** — nightly rates, occupancy patterns, platform fees, cleaning, utilities, furnishings, licensing
- 🔑 **Long-term rental** — monthly rent, vacancy risk, tenant defaults, property tax, condo fees, insurance, maintenance reserves
- 🔨 **Buy-renovate-resell** — timeline-based analysis of purchase → renovation costs → holding costs → sale, with capital-gains tax
- 📝 **Off-plan assignment** — buying pre-completion and selling the contractual position to another buyer, measured against actual deposits rather than full price

### ⚙️ One engine underneath

All four strategies run through a single shared financial model that handles
acquisition costs, transfer tax, mortgage terms and your investor tax profile
*identically* across every scenario. That is what makes the comparison honest:
the strategies differ because the strategies differ, not because each one was
given its own quietly flattering assumptions.

---

## 📊 Ranges, Not Point Estimates

No single set of inputs is ever right, so nothing is reported as one confident
number.

- **Three cases in parallel** — pessimistic, base and optimistic
- **Tornado charts** — which assumptions actually drive the outcome, ranked
- **Monte Carlo (optional)** — the *probability* the deal clears your target return

> ⚠️ This is the part that changes decisions. It is common for a deal to look
> best as a short-term rental in the base case and worst in the downside,
> purely because the model is levered to occupancy.

---

## 🔍 The Comparables Engine

This is the innovation. DealScope automatically fetches comparable properties
on the same streets, matched by rule rather than by eye — location, typology,
area, condition and listing date.

A property's numbers mean nothing in isolation; they only become a judgement
when set against what else the same money buys nearby. So every assumption is
grounded in real local market data instead of a seeded guess — and every
derived number stays visible and editable, with the sample it came from
attached.

### 📐 Reported for every strategy

Net yield · cash-on-cash return · IRR · NPV · payback period · equity multiple

...plus the strategy-specific break-even point: the occupancy rate, monthly
rent or resale price at which the deal stops making money.

## 📄 Output: A Real Report

Not a dashboard that disappears when the tab closes.

- 📋 Full assumption set and data sources
- ⚖️ Strategy comparison table
- 💧 Per-scenario cash-flow waterfalls
- 📉 Sensitivity charts and break-even analysis
- ✅ A plain-language verdict, *with conditions*

> **Example verdict:** works as a long-term rental at the asking price —
> works as a short-term rental only above 65% occupancy.

---

## 👥 Two Audiences, Opposite Sides of the Table

- 🏢 **Estate agents** — an advisory tool: educate the client with data rather than photos
- 🧾 **Buyers** — negotiating ammunition: comparables ranked by price, competing offers, asking-price percentile

Serving both only works if the tool refuses to take a side. The report says the
same thing about a property regardless of who ran it, because the instant a
conclusion depends on who paid for it, it is worth nothing to either party.

- ⏳ Retention windows rather than indefinite storage
- 🔀 Identifying data separated from deal metrics, so aggregate market insight can be published without exposing anyone
- 🗑️ Deletion requests honoured as a working feature, not a paragraph in a policy

### 🌍 Jurisdiction-specific rules

Tax and regulatory parameters — transfer tax, capital gains, rental income
rules, licensing — are configurable per location and updated as the law
changes.

---

## 🧭 Design Philosophy

> The tool shows all its work. Every assumption is visible, sourced and
> editable.

It is explicitly a modeling aid and **not financial advice**, designed so that
a professional reviewing the report can see exactly which number to challenge
— rather than being asked to trust a black-box conclusion.
