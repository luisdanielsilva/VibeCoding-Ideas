> 🏠 **DealScope answers one question:** given this property at this price,
> which strategy makes the most money — and how badly does that answer change
> if the assumptions are wrong?

It never fetches a page. Every number about the local market arrives from
**PropInsight (VC-005)** through a two-call API, or is typed in by hand.
DealScope's entire job is what happens *after* the evidence is on the table.

## 🏘️ Five Strategies, One Engine

| Strategy | Driven by | For |
|---|---|---|
| 🛏️ **Short-term rental** | Nightly rate, occupancy curve, platform fees, cleaning, furnishing, licensing | Buyer |
| 🔑 **Long-term rental** | Monthly rent, vacancy, default risk, tax, condo fees, maintenance | Buyer |
| 🔨 **Buy-renovate-resell** | Works per m², contingency, holding costs, selling costs, capital gains | Both |
| 📝 **Off-plan assignment** | Deposits actually paid in, consent fee, transfer-tax saving, completion date | Buyer |
| 🏗️ **Develop & sell units** | Build cost per m², unit mix, price ladder, sell-out schedule, finance during works | Developer |

The shared engine is what makes the comparison honest: strategies differ
because the strategies differ, not because each was handed its own quietly
flattering assumptions.

## 📐 The Developer's Question: Residual Land Value

A developer does not ask "what return does this give?" — they ask **"what is
the most I can pay and still make my margin?"** So the model runs backwards:

```
   target margin (say 20%)
        │
        ▼
   gross development value          ← unit mix × price/m² from comps
        −  build cost               ← per m² × buildable area
        −  professional fees
        −  finance during works     ← driven by the sell-out schedule
        −  selling costs & tax
        −  target profit
        ═══════════════════════════
        = 💰 maximum site price
```

That single figure is what a developer takes to a negotiation. Everything
above it is a loss dressed as a project.

---

## ⏱️ Sell-Out Schedule — Where the Pairing Pays Off

Finance costs on a development are a function of **how long the units take to
sell**, which is a function of the local absorption rate. That rate is only
observable by watching listings disappear over months — precisely what
PropInsight's snapshot history records.

| Absorption (units/month) | 20 units sold in | Finance cost impact |
|---|---|---|
| 4.0 | 5 months | 🟢 Baseline |
| 2.0 | 10 months | 🟠 Roughly doubles |
| 1.0 | 20 months | 🔴 Often fatal |

> ⚠️ This is the number developers guess and the one that most often sinks a
> project. Guessing it from a single scrape is impossible; measuring it is
> what VC-005 exists for.

---

## 📊 Ranges, Not Point Estimates

- **Three cases in parallel** — pessimistic, base, optimistic
- **Tornado chart** — which assumptions actually drive the outcome, ranked
- **Monte Carlo (optional)** — the *probability* of clearing the target return

> ⚠️ A deal commonly looks best as a short-term rental in the base case and
> worst in the downside, purely because the model is levered to occupancy.
> One number would hide that completely.

---

## 📐 Reported for Every Strategy

Net yield · cash-on-cash · IRR · NPV · payback · equity multiple

...plus the strategy-specific break-even: the occupancy rate, monthly rent,
resale price or sales pace at which the deal stops making money.

## 📄 Output: A Report, Not a Dashboard

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| 📋 Assumptions | Every input, with its source and sample size |
| ⚖️ Comparison | All strategies on identical metrics |
| 💧 Cash flow | Per-scenario waterfalls + amortization |
| 📉 Sensitivity | Tornado + break-even charts |
| ✅ Verdict | Plain language, **with conditions** |

> **Buyer verdict:** works as a long-term rental at asking — works as a
> short-term rental only above 65% occupancy.
>
> **Developer verdict:** viable at a site price up to €420k, assuming
> absorption holds at 3 units/month; below 1.5 it stops clearing the margin.

The report is the deliverable because these get forwarded — to a partner, an
accountant, a lender. A document with its assumptions on page one is something
another person can actually argue with.

## ⚠️ Risks

- 🎭 **Garbage in, confident garbage out** — a polished PDF lends false authority to bad inputs. Assumptions must stay visible on every page.
- 🏷️ **Comparables are asking prices, not sold prices** — systematically optimistic, and the report must say so every time
- 📉 Thin or scattered comp sets can't support a verdict — decline rather than guess
- 🏚️ Renovation and build costs are where flip and develop models break down; they need real quotes, not per-m² averages
- 📜 Tax rules are jurisdiction-specific and change with every budget
- 🚫 Short-let licensing is tightening in many cities and can invalidate a strategy outright
- ⚖️ Must not read as financial advice — modelling aid, assumptions shown
- 🤝 Pressure to add an "agent mode" that flatters a listing would destroy the credibility both audiences depend on

## ✅ Definition of Done

**A buyer** pastes a listing URL and gets a ranked strategy comparison, a
fair-value band, and the price above which the deal stops working.

**A developer** enters a site and a build programme and gets the maximum they
can pay for it, the sell-out schedule behind that figure, and the absorption
rate at which the margin disappears.

Both reports print every assumption, name its source, and say plainly when the
evidence was too thin to conclude.
